How to judge a strategy result

  1. Check the inputs: source, timestamp, market session, corporate-action basis, quote freshness and whether a fill actually occurred.
  2. Keep the sequence honest: choose rules using prior outcomes, then test later data. Repeatedly tuning on a test period stops it being an untouched test.
  3. Measure after costs: include spread, slippage, fees and borrow costs where applicable. A high win rate can still lose money.
  4. Read the uncertainty: review session-day coverage, clustered intervals and regime changes, not only the number of trades. Compare a matched benchmark before claiming outperformance.

Bayesian estimate versus research policy

With 14 wins in 20 trials and a Beta(5, 5) prior, the ordinary posterior mean is 63.33%. This app's policy removes the shrinkage discount at 20 samples and uses 70% for its adjusted research score. That policy score should not be read as a calibrated probability of the next trade winning.
