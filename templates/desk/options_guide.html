Buy to open a call

You pay a premium for the right to buy the stock at the strike price. A rising stock generally helps, but time decay and changes in implied volatility also affect the option price.

To exit a call you own, sell the same contract to close. You do not need to exercise it to close the option.

  1. Choose the exact contract

    Stock, call or put, expiration date, strike price. A different date or strike is a different position.

  2. Check the total dollars

    Premium × 100 × contracts, then fees. $1.20 is $120 for one standard contract before fees.

  3. Preview, practice, then close

    Inspect the bid/ask and modeled loss. Confirm only the paper review, then manage the position in the paper book.

Four actions that are easy to mix up

Buy to open
Pay for a new call or put. The purchased option premium can be lost in full.
Sell to close
Sell a call or put you already own. This exits that long contract.
Sell to open
Create a short option position and receive premium. A short call can require delivery of shares; a short put can require buying shares if assigned. This app practices these only as paired, protected spreads.
Buy to close
Buy back a short option to exit it before assignment. Closing a spread reverses both legs together.
Strike and expiration
Strike is the contract's exercise price; expiration is the date the contract ends. Neither is your entry premium.
Bid, ask and break-even
Bid is a buyer's quote; ask is a seller's quote. Expiry break-even describes the modeled outcome at expiration, not the stock move needed to sell the option profitably today.
