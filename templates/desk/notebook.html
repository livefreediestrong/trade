Plain-English guide · what do these terms mean?

Paper / live
Paper uses pretend money in a local book. Live sends an order to your real broker after approval.
Market order
Buy or sell at the next available prices. It prioritizes execution; the price can move and there is no price cap.
Limit order · DAY
Buy at your limit or less, or sell at your limit or more. It may never fill. DAY means the remaining order expires at the end of its trading day.
Fractional share / partial fill
A fractional share is part of one share, such as 0.1 shares. A partial fill means only some of an order has traded; the rest may still be working.
P&L / realized / unrealized
Profit and loss. Realized comes from closed trades; unrealized is the current gain or loss on open holdings.
Notional / buying power
Notional is shares multiplied by price, before fees. Buying power is the broker's available trading capacity, not a recommended spending amount.
Spread / slippage / basis points
Spread is the gap between quoted buy and sell prices. Slippage is the difference between expected and actual fill price. One basis point is 0.01%.
Stop / target / short selling
A stop is an exit trigger, not a guaranteed price. A target is a hoped-for exit. Short selling borrows shares and sells them first; rising prices and borrowing costs can cause large losses.
Confidence / sample / calibration
AI confidence is its own estimate, not a proven chance of winning. A sample counts observations. Calibration compares stated confidence with outcomes.
Research memory / weights
Memory stores observations used as evidence. Training updates model parameters from that evidence. Here, scored outcomes update a small strategy-ranking model; stored notes alone do not retrain the language model.
